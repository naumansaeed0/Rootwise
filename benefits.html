01PROMISING, WITH LIMITS

A little nature, indoors.

Some studies report benefits for mood, comfort, or stress-related measures around indoor plants. Results vary with the setting, the people involved, and the research method.

Research using plant images can tell us about preferences, but cannot establish that a real plant will improve someone’s health. Plants complement everyday wellbeing; they are not treatment.

02A PERSONAL EXPERIENCE

A habit worth keeping.

Noticing new growth, checking soil, and tending a living thing can create an enjoyable routine. The benefit is personal: there is no universal dose of plant care or guaranteed productivity boost.

Choose plants that suit your energy, budget, and light. A healthy plant you enjoy is a better starting point than a roomful you struggle to maintain.

03LET’S CLEAR THE AIR

Your pothos is a plant.
It isn’t an air purifier.

Plants can remove some pollutants in sealed laboratory chambers. That does not translate into meaningful air cleaning from a few houseplants in a normally ventilated home.

A review by Cummings and Waring found that passive potted plants remove volatile organic compounds too slowly to compete with typical building air exchange at practical plant densities.

For air quality, address pollutant sources, ventilation, and appropriate filtration. Enjoy your plants for what they bring to daily life.

Read the Cummings & Waring review

Keep the whole picture in mind.

Some plants are toxic to pets or irritating to people. Damp potting mix can support fungus gnats, and flowers or mold can bother sensitive individuals. A little care and a good species choice go a long way.
