01A LITTLE UNDERSTANDING GOES A LONG WAY

Help it feel at home.

Light

Place near a bright window with filtered light. Avoid intense midday sun; acclimate slowly when changing position.

Light, explained

Watering

Let the upper third of the mix dry before watering. Water through, then drain completely; avoid keeping roots continuously wet.

Humidity

Ordinary indoor humidity is usually sufficient. Keep away from heating vents and cold, drying drafts.

Temperature

Most comfortable at 18–27°C. Avoid frost, sudden temperature changes, and cold window glass.

Soil

A well-draining indoor potting mix, with added perlite or fine bark for air around the roots.

Fertilizing

Feed lightly with a balanced houseplant fertilizer at the label dilution during active growth. Pause feeding when growth slows; never fertilize dry or damaged roots.

Repotting

Check roots during active growth. Repot only when crowded or the mix has broken down, using a pot just one size larger with drainage.

Propagation

Stem or leaf cuttings.

02WHEN SOMETHING FEELS OFF

Common problems, considered.

Yellowing leaves and wet mix

Check drainage and root health. Allow the mix to dry to this plant’s preferred level; remove soft, rotting roots before repotting if needed.

Pests on leaves or stems

Isolate the plant and inspect undersides. Remove visible pests; use a labeled treatment suited to the pest and repeat according to its instructions.

Leggy or weak growth

Move gradually toward a brighter suitable position. Rotate for even growth; do not compensate for low light with extra fertilizer.

Children & handling

Not a food plant. Keep out of reach of small children; ingestion can cause discomfort and small parts can be a choking risk.

If ingestion is suspected, contact a veterinarian or local poison service promptly. Do not use this guide for diagnosis.
