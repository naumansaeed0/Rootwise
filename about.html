An independent, static field guide for people who want to understand their indoor plants.

01FIELD NOTE

Good information should feel approachable.

Rootwise brings together 55 popular indoor plants and six evergreen care guides. This is a reference library, not a blog or a shop. There are no accounts, contributions, trackers, or personalized health claims. All content ships with the site.

The design pairs botanical forms with the quiet precision of a field instrument. Every illustration is custom SVG. They are stylized studies, not photographs or tools for identifying an unknown plant.

02FIELD NOTE

Where the knowledge comes from.

Species profiles draw on university extension and botanical resources. Taxonomic synonyms are retained where nursery labels and references use different names. General care advice is a starting point, not a universal watering formula.

  • NC State Extension Gardener Plant Toolbox — species descriptions, cultivation, and safety references.
  • University of Florida IFAS — houseplant care fundamentals.
  • University of Minnesota Extension — indoor light.
  • ASPCA plant database — cat and dog toxicity; exact species matters.
  • Royal Horticultural Society — wellbeing research and its limits.
  • Cummings & Waring, 2019/2020 — review of passive plant air-cleaning claims.
  • 2022 scoping review — indoor plants and mental health.

Each plant links to its supporting species or genus source. Genus-level sources are identified as such. Data notes distinguish unresolved details from reviewed material.

03FIELD NOTE

A careful note on safety.

“Pet-friendly” means listed as non-toxic to cats and dogs, not safe to eat. Any plant material may cause gastrointestinal upset. Toxicity to other animals can differ. This site does not provide veterinary or medical advice.

Keep plants and loose parts away from small children. Do not eat or use a plant medicinally based on this guide. For suspected ingestion, contact a veterinarian or your local poison service; do not wait for symptoms or rely on an illustration for identification.

Size groups describe typical indoor scale: small plants fit a shelf, medium plants need more room or trailing space, and large plants may need floor space and pruning. Conditions and cultivar can change the mature size substantially.
